The concept

A modular accessory designed to fit over an existing rucksack without permanently altering the pack. Flexible rods radiate from a lightweight support structure while open mesh helps stabilize spacing and distribute loads.

Over-pack mounting

Top, side and lower webbing straps secure the accessory around the existing pack.

Modular structure

Short removable rod sections make storage, replacement and prototype adjustment easier.

Flexible geometry

Rod spacing can be adjusted around the pack while keeping ends blunt and flexible for safer prototyping.

Prototype assembly

High-level prototype guidance for a non-electrified, non-weaponized demonstration model.

Suggested prototype parts

  • Flexible fiberglass driveway-marker style rods or flexible plastic tubing
  • Plastic garden/utility mesh
  • Nylon webbing straps with side-release buckles
  • Blunt rubber/plastic end caps
  • Reusable cable ties or non-sharp plastic clips
  • Lightweight flexible tubing/hoop material for an outer support frame
  • Foam padding at pack contact points

Assembly sequence

Fit the harness.

Arrange webbing over the rucksack so loads are shared across the top, sides and bottom.

Add the support frame.

Secure a lightweight flexible perimeter structure to the harness.

Add flexible rods.

Attach blunt-ended rods around the support structure, keeping them flexible and removable.

Stabilize with mesh.

Loosely secure mesh to maintain spacing without making a rigid cage.

Fit-test.

Check visibility, snagging, balance, emergency removal and clearance before any demonstration.

Prototype safety

Do not electrify the assembly or use sharpened, conductive, projectile-like or rigid penetrating tips. Keep clear of aircraft and rotating machinery. Prototype at walking speed in a controlled area and ensure the wearer can remove it quickly.

Design instructions from the concept

A practical interpretation of the illustrated design using ordinary, non-sharp prototype materials. Dimensions should be adjusted to the actual rucksack and wearer rather than treated as fixed engineering tolerances.

1. Over-ruck harness

Start with two vertical nylon webbing runs down the rear face of the ruck and connect them with upper, middle and lower cross-straps. Route the straps around the pack—not through the wearer’s shoulder harness—and use side-release buckles so the entire prototype can be removed.

2. Lightweight perimeter frame

Form a flexible oval/rounded support frame slightly larger than the rear face of the pack using flexible plastic tubing or similar lightweight material. Secure it to the webbing at several points so the load is spread over the ruck instead of hanging from one attachment.

3. Mesh carrier

Cut plastic utility/garden mesh to follow the support frame. Attach it loosely with reusable plastic ties or clips, leaving enough movement for the frame to flex. Keep the center area accessible for ruck zippers and pockets where practical.

4. Radial pole layout

Arrange flexible green/black rods around the frame in staggered rows, pointing outward in different directions to reproduce the dense 360° visual effect shown in the concept. Use blunt capped ends and flexible attachment loops. Do not use sharpened or conductive tips.

5. Modular pole sections

For a packable prototype, use short removable sections joined with ordinary non-sharp plastic couplers. Keep individual rods light and flexible. Alternate green and black sections to match the concept styling.

6. Stabilization

Add secondary mesh loops or flexible ties partway along groups of rods where needed to reduce excessive movement. The rods should remain individually separated rather than being rigidly joined at their outer ends.

7. Weight distribution

Use multiple upper, side and lower attachment points. Add foam where the accessory contacts the pack. Keep heavier connectors close to the ruck and lighter components farther outward to reduce leverage on the wearer.

8. Final fit check

With the ruck stationary, verify all ends are blunt, straps cannot interfere with shoulder/waist-belt operation, the wearer can remove the ruck normally, and the assembly does not block vision or snag nearby people. Test only as a non-electrified display/prototype in a controlled area.

Concept parts list

  • Nylon webbing and side-release buckles
  • Flexible plastic tubing for the perimeter frame
  • Plastic utility/garden mesh
  • Flexible fiberglass marker rods or plastic rods
  • Blunt rubber/plastic end caps
  • Plastic couplers for removable sections
  • Reusable cable ties / plastic clips
  • Closed-cell foam padding
Design boundary: This is a non-electrified, blunt-ended prototype/display design. It does not include sharpened tips, high voltage, entanglement mechanisms, or instructions intended to damage drones, aircraft, people, or property.
